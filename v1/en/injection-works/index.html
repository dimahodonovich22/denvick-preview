Warning signs

When is injection needed?

Injection is the solution when a structure is sound at its core, but cracks, voids or leaks are affecting its strength or watertightness.

Cracks in floors or walls

Structural cracks in concrete that weaken the cohesion of an element or keep growing.

Leaking basement or car park

Water getting in through cracks, construction joints or penetrations in basements, car parks and lift pits.

Hollow or subsided floor slab

A floor that sounds hollow, tilts or cracks because the ground beneath has washed away or settled.

Damp and efflorescence

Wet patches, lime deposits or rust stains that show water is finding its way through the concrete.

What are injection works?

In injection works we introduce a liquid product under pressure into cracks, voids or pores in a structure. That product cures exactly where it is needed: in the crack itself, under a floor slab or in the wall. That way we restore strength or watertightness without demolition. It is a targeted technique that often makes a much larger intervention unnecessary.

Three types of injection

Structural injection with epoxy. A crack interrupts the flow of forces in a concrete element. By filling the crack with a liquid epoxy resin, the concrete becomes one whole again. Epoxy bonds excellently to concrete, and once cured the bond is usually stronger than the surrounding material.

Waterproofing injection with polyurethane. If water leaks through a crack, a construction joint or around a pipe, we inject polyurethane. It stays flexible, absorbs limited movement and closes off the water path for good. For active leaks we use a fast foaming variant that also works while water is flowing through the crack.

Void filling with cement. Voids under a concrete floor are caused by washout or settlement of the ground beneath. Through drill holes in the floor we inject a cement slurry that fills the voids. The floor slab is supported over its full surface again, and cracking caused by deflection stops.

The cause first, then the product

An injection resolves the effect, but not always the cause. A crack caused by settlement or an overloaded floor will return if nothing else changes. That is why we first look at why the crack is there. Sometimes an injection is enough, sometimes strengthening or concrete repair is needed. We weigh that up together with you or your engineering firm, based on what we find on site.

Little disruption during the works

Injection is carried out through small drill holes. No demolition is involved and the work zone stays small. As a result, a basement, car park or production hall usually remains in normal use during the works. For urgent leaks our response team is ready, also outside office hours.

Our approach

How injection works

Every crack and every leak is different. That is why we only choose the injection material after a thorough analysis of the cause.

  1. 01

    Analysing the crack or leak

    We determine whether a crack is still moving, whether it is dry or carrying water and how deep it runs. Where needed, with concrete testing.

    Concrete testing and inspection
  2. 02

    Choosing the injection material

    Epoxy resin for structural bonding, polyurethane for flexible waterproofing, cement to fill voids.

  3. 03

    Drilling and fitting packers

    Along the crack we drill angled holes into the core and fit injection packers at fixed intervals.

  4. 04

    Injecting under pressure

    With an injection pump we introduce the product at controlled pressure until it emerges at the next packer.

  5. 05

    Finishing

    After curing we remove the packers and neatly finish the drill holes and the surface.

  6. 06

    Inspection

    We check the watertightness or the fill and document the result, so you know exactly what has been done.

Frequently asked questions

Questions about injection works

Your question is not listed? Ask us directly.

What is the difference between epoxy and polyurethane injection?

Epoxy resin cures rock hard and bonds a crack structurally, so the element becomes one whole again. Polyurethane stays flexible or foams on contact with water. That makes it ideal for sealing a leaking crack that may still move.

Can you inject while water is coming in?

Yes. For active leaks we use polyurethane that foams on contact with water and closes off the path. Where needed, a second injection follows for a durable seal.

What is void filling under a floor slab?

When the ground under a concrete floor has washed away or settled, voids form. Through holes in the floor we inject a cement slurry that fills the voids, so the floor bears on its full surface again.

Does the floor or wall have to be broken open for this?

No. We work through small drill holes, so no demolition is needed. The work zone stays small and the building usually remains in normal use.

Will an injected crack come back?

If the cause of the crack has been addressed, a structural injection is durable. For cracks that keep moving, we choose a flexible product that can absorb that movement.

How much do injection works cost?

That depends on the length and width of the cracks, the chosen product and access. After a site visit, you will receive a clear, free quote with no obligation.
