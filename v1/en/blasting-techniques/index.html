When to blast?

Blasting is almost always the first step of a durable repair or finish. These are the situations in which we bring in the blasting equipment.

Removing rust

Derusting steel down to bare metal, so that a new coating adheres and protects for years.

Removing old layers

Removing paint, coatings, adhesive residues and graffiti from concrete, steel and masonry.

Opening up the substrate

Blasting away the weak top layer of a concrete floor before a poured floor or coating.

Cleaning facades

Cleaning soiled facades and masonry with a technique that does not damage the surface.

Why blasting?

Anyone who wants to coat a concrete floor, protect steel or carry out a repair has to start with a clean, sound substrate. A new layer will not adhere to rust, old paint or a weak top layer. Blasting removes those layers thoroughly and at the same time gives the surface the right roughness for a coating or mortar to bond firmly. That is why it is the first step of almost every durable repair.

The right technique for every substrate

Sandblasting and grit blasting are powerful and suitable for steel and concrete. Grit creates less dust than sand and leaves a rough profile to which a coating adheres well.

Water jetting at high pressure removes dirt, loose material and weak concrete without any abrasive. In wet blasting, we mix sand with water, which greatly reduces dust.

Shot blasting is what we use for concrete floors. Steel shot opens up the surface and is immediately vacuumed up and reused, so that hardly any dust is released.

Ice blasting works with dry ice that evaporates on contact. No abrasive is left behind, which makes it suitable for sensitive surfaces and for spaces that must not be contaminated.

Testing before we blast

Blasting too gently leaves rust and old layers behind; blasting too hard damages the surface. That is why we first test the technique and the pressure on a test area. This shows us in advance which result is achievable, and we choose the approach that cleans the surface without harming it.

Straight on to the next step

Blasted steel soon starts to rust again. That is why we plan the blasting so that the primer or coating follows shortly afterwards, applied by the same crew. That saves time, coordination and surprises, and the result is a protective layer on a substrate we prepared ourselves.

Our approach

How we work

Every substrate calls for a different technique and a different abrasive. Too gentle does not work, too harsh causes damage. That is why we test first.

  1. 01

    Assessing the substrate

    We look at the material, the layers to be removed and the result needed for the next step.

  2. 02

    Test area

    On a small area, we test abrasive and pressure, so that we are sure of the result and do not damage the surface.

  3. 03

    Screening off the work zone

    We screen off the surroundings against dust and abrasive, and protect windows, installations and anything that must not be blasted.

  4. 04

    Blasting

    With the chosen technique, we blast the surface to the required cleanliness and roughness.

  5. 05

    Cleaning up

    We collect the abrasive and the removed layers and dispose of them in line with the regulations.

  6. 06

    Ready for the next step

    The surface is immediately ready for repair, coating or a poured floor, ideally without delay so that steel does not rust again.

Frequently asked questions

Questions about blasting work

Your question is not listed? Ask us directly.

Which blasting techniques do you use?

Sandblasting and grit blasting for robust substrates, high pressure water jetting, wet blasting with sand and water to limit dust, shot blasting for concrete floors and ice blasting for sensitive surfaces. The choice depends on the substrate and the desired result.

Does blasting create a lot of dust?

Dry blasting creates dust, which is why we screen off the work zone. Where dust really has to be kept to a minimum, we choose wet blasting, shot blasting with extraction or ice blasting, which leaves almost no residual waste.

What is ice blasting?

In ice blasting, we use dry ice pellets as the abrasive. They evaporate on contact, so no abrasive is left behind. It is gentle on the surface and suitable for sensitive substrates and installations.

Can every surface be blasted?

Almost every surface, but not with every technique. Soft masonry or natural stone calls for a different approach than steel or concrete. That is why we always work with a test area.

What happens to the blasting waste?

We collect the abrasive and the removed layers and dispose of them. With old paint layers that may contain lead or other hazardous substances, we take the necessary measures for safe working and disposal.

How much does blasting cost?

That depends on the surface area, the substrate, the layers to be removed and the accessibility. After a site visit, you will receive a clear, free quote with no obligation.
