Warning signs

Does this sound familiar?

Damp in a basement or car park is often tolerated for a long time, until rust appears in the concrete or the space becomes unusable. These are the signs to take action.

Water when it rains

Water coming in after rain or with a high water table, through cracks, construction joints or the junction between floor and wall.

Damp walls and floors

Dark patches, mould or a musty smell in basements and storage rooms that never fully dry out.

Leaking penetrations

Water along pipes, cables or drains that pass through a basement wall or floor.

Efflorescence and rust

White deposits and rust stains on concrete show that water has been seeping through for a long time and is attacking the reinforcement.

Water always finds a way

Concrete looks solid, but every crack, construction joint and penetration is a potential path for water. Groundwater and rainwater exert pressure on basement walls and floors and seek out the weakest point. Once water is inside, it does not stop at a wet floor: it carries salts, attacks the reinforcement and turns a dry storage room into a space you can no longer use.

From the inside or from the outside

Waterproofing on the outside stops the water before it reaches the structure. In new construction, that is the logical choice, but in an existing building it means excavating, which is often expensive or impossible. That is why we usually work from the inside in existing basements and car parks. With injections, we seal cracks and joints, and with a cementitious or crystalline waterproofing, we make the wall itself impermeable. Which combination is needed depends on the water pressure, the type of wall and how the space is used.

The weak spots: joints and penetrations

Most leaks do not occur in the middle of a wall, but at the transitions. The junction between floor and wall, a construction joint between two concrete pours or a pipe running through the basement wall: that is where water most often gets in. We therefore always give these details separate treatment, with flexible seals or injections that can follow movements in the structure.

A dry structure is a healthy structure

Waterproofing is not only about comfort. Water that seeps through concrete for years carries chlorides and oxygen to the reinforcement and so sets concrete decay in motion. Timely waterproofing therefore also protects the load capacity of your building. Where damage has already begun, we combine the waterproofing with a structural concrete repair.

Our approach

How we make a structure watertight

Waterproofing only holds when it suits both the path the water takes and the structure. That is why we always start with the cause.

  1. 01

    Tracing the water

    We determine where and how the water gets in: through cracks, joints, penetrations or the concrete itself. If in doubt, with a concrete investigation.

    Concrete testing and inspection
  2. 02

    Stopping leaks

    We seal active leaks with an injection that foams on contact with water, so that work can continue in dry conditions.

    Injection works
  3. 03

    Sealing joints and penetrations

    Construction joints, expansion joints and penetrations receive a flexible seal that absorbs movements in the structure.

    Joint sealing
  4. 04

    Repairing the substrate

    We first repair damaged concrete and corroding reinforcement, so that the waterproofing goes onto a sound substrate.

    Concrete repair
  5. 05

    Applying a waterproof layer

    Depending on the situation, a cementitious or crystalline waterproofing, a liquid membrane or a coating.

  6. 06

    Inspection and handover

    We check the watertightness and document what was carried out, so that you know what is in your structure.

Frequently asked questions

Questions about waterproofing

Your question is not listed? Ask us directly.

Can a basement be waterproofed from the inside?

In many cases, yes. With injections and a cementitious or crystalline waterproofing on the inside, we keep water out without excavating the outside. Whether that is enough depends on the water pressure and the condition of the wall.

What is the difference between rising damp and water ingress?

Rising damp is drawn up from the ground through the pores of masonry. Water ingress comes in from outside through cracks, joints or penetrations, often during rain or when the water table is high. Each problem calls for a different approach.

How long does waterproofing last?

Properly executed waterproofing on a sound substrate lasts many years. It is important that the joints and penetrations are treated as well, because that is where most leaks occur.

Does the basement need to be empty during the works?

The area where we work must be clear. For the rest, we plan around your situation, so that you can use the space again as soon as possible.

How much does waterproofing cost?

That depends on the surface area, the cause of the water and the chosen technique. After a site visit, you will receive a clear, free quote with no obligation.

Which region do you cover?

From Bornem, we work throughout Flanders and Brussels, and for larger projects across the rest of the Benelux.
