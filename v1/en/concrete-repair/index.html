Warning signs

Do you recognise this?

Concrete decay starts invisibly and only shows once the damage is well under way. These are the signs you should not ignore.

Spalling concrete

Pieces of concrete coming away from columns, balconies, beams or facade elements. Often the first visible sign of corroding reinforcement.

Corroding reinforcement

Brown rust stains or exposed bars. Rust takes up several times the volume of steel and breaks the concrete apart from the inside.

Cracks along the reinforcement

Hairline or wider cracks let water, CO₂ and salts reach the steel. This speeds up the deterioration.

Damp patches and efflorescence

White lime deposits, wet patches or dripping water show that moisture is passing through the concrete. That moisture is what drives concrete decay.

What is concrete decay?

Reinforced concrete is strong because two materials work together: the concrete takes the compression, the steel reinforcement takes the tensile forces. As long as the concrete around the steel is strongly alkaline, the steel stays protected against corrosion. That protection is lost over the years. CO₂ from the air slowly penetrates the concrete and lowers its alkalinity. This is called carbonation. Where there is road salt or sea air, chlorides do the same, but faster and more locally.

Once the protective layer around the reinforcement has gone and moisture gets in, the steel starts to rust. Rust takes up several times the volume of the original steel. That expansion pushes the concrete away from the inside: first hairline cracks appear, then pieces spall off and the reinforcement becomes exposed. This process is what we call concrete decay.

Why acting quickly pays off

Concrete decay does not stop by itself. Every crack lets in more water and oxygen, which makes the corrosion progress faster. What is a local repair to one balcony edge or column today becomes work on the entire element within a few years, at a higher cost and sometimes with a stability problem.

With facades, balconies and canopies, safety is also at stake: falling pieces of concrete can hit people below. As an owner or property manager, you are responsible for those risks. A timely inspection gives you certainty about the condition of the structure and a sound repair plan.

Structural repair, not camouflage

Filling a hole with cement mortar looks like a quick fix, but if the corroding reinforcement is not treated, the concrete will spall again after a short time. That is why we always carry out structural repairs: remove the damaged concrete, clean and protect the reinforcement, rebuild the element with a repair mortar suited to the load and finally protect the concrete against new deterioration.

The European standard EN 1504 classifies repair mortars according to their strength and bond. For load bearing elements we choose a mortar that can help carry the forces in the element. For aesthetic repairs to facades we choose a mortar that matches the existing concrete in colour and texture.

From a single balcony to an entire car park

Concrete repair is not standard work. A balcony for a homeowner calls for a different approach than the columns of a production hall that cannot stop for a single day, or a quay wall that can only be reached from the water. With our own crews and our own equipment, from scaffolding and aerial platforms to blasting units, we adapt the method to the project, with one point of contact from inspection to handover.

Our approach

How we approach concrete repair

A repair only lasts if the cause is tackled too. That is why every project goes through the same steps, from investigation to protection.

  1. 01

    Inspection and concrete testing

    We map the damage: how deep does the deterioration go, how much concrete cover is left and are chlorides present? Where needed, using core samples and measurements.

    Concrete testing and inspection
  2. 02

    Removing damaged concrete

    Loose and damaged concrete is broken out or blasted away down to sound concrete, and the reinforcement is exposed all round.

    Blasting techniques
  3. 03

    Treating the reinforcement

    The steel is cleaned of rust and treated with a corrosion inhibiting primer. Where too much steel has been lost, we add extra reinforcement.

  4. 04

    Reprofiling

    Using a structural repair mortar based on cement or epoxy, we build the element back up to its original shape and concrete cover.

  5. 05

    Strengthening where needed

    If the element has to carry more than before, or its load capacity has been reduced, we strengthen it with bonded reinforcement in carbon fibre or steel.

    Bonded reinforcement
  6. 06

    Protection

    A protective coating or impregnation keeps water, CO₂ and chlorides out, so the repair lasts for years.

    Protective coatings

Frequently asked questions

Questions about concrete repair

Your question is not listed? Ask us directly.

What exactly is concrete decay?

Concrete decay is the general term for damage that occurs when the steel reinforcement in concrete corrodes. The rust expands, causing the concrete to crack and spall from the inside. The cause is usually carbonation, where CO₂ from the air makes the concrete lose its protective alkalinity, or chlorides from road salt or sea air.

How do I know whether my concrete needs repairing?

Spalled pieces, rust stains, cracks along the reinforcement and damp patches are clear signs. If in doubt, we come and take a look on site and use concrete testing to determine how far the deterioration extends.

How much does concrete repair cost?

That depends on the extent of the damage, access (scaffolding, aerial work platform or rope access) and whether strengthening or a protective coating is needed. That is why we do not work with standard prices. After a site visit, you will receive a clear, free quote with no obligation.

How long does a concrete repair last?

When the cause is tackled and the concrete is protected afterwards, a structural repair lasts for many years. A superficial patch that leaves the reinforcement untreated holds up for a much shorter time. That is why we do the job thoroughly.

Can the building stay in use during the works?

In most cases, yes. We work in phases, screen off the work zone and plan around your activities.

Do you also work for homeowners?

Yes. We repair a single balcony or basement for homeowners as well as large structures for companies, owners' associations and public bodies.

Which region do you cover?

From Bornem we work throughout Flanders and Brussels, and on larger projects in the rest of the Benelux.
