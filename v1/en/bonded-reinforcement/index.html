Applications

When to strengthen?

Strengthening a structure is needed when the load increases or the load capacity decreases. Bonded reinforcement is then often the fastest and lightest solution.

Heavier loads

A new machine, heavier racking, a green roof or a change of use for the building calls for more load capacity.

New openings

A stairwell, service penetration or doorway in a floor or wall interrupts the reinforcement. We strengthen the element again around the opening.

Lost reinforcement

Concrete decay has corroded away part of the steel. After the concrete repair, we restore the missing strength.

Design or construction errors

An element that proves too weak in a check calculation can be strengthened without replacing it.

Strengthening without demolition

A concrete beam, floor or column has a certain load capacity. If the load increases, or strength has been lost, there are two traditional solutions: replace the element or make it heavier with extra concrete and steel. Both are drastic. Bonded reinforcement is a third way. We bond thin, very strong reinforcement to the surface of the existing element, so that it can carry more without anything being demolished.

Carbon, aramid or steel

Carbon laminates are thin, stiff strips that we bond to the tension side of beams and floors. They take over part of the tensile forces and so increase the bending strength.

Carbon fibre fabric is flexible and follows any shape. We wrap it around columns to confine them, or apply it where a stiff strip will not fit.

Aramid and hybrid fabrics combine strength with greater elongation. They are used where a structure must be able to absorb impacts or vibrations.

Steel plates are the traditional form of bonded reinforcement and remain a good choice where fire resistance or existing detailing is the deciding factor.

Light, thin and fast

Carbon weighs a fraction of steel and does not rust. Strengthening therefore adds hardly any weight or thickness. Ceiling heights are maintained, finishes can simply go over it and the works themselves are quick. For a production hall or a car park, that means less downtime. For a home, it means a renovation can go ahead without heavy steel beams having to be brought inside.

Always based on a calculation

Strengthening is structural work. How much reinforcement is needed, where it should go and how it is anchored follows from a calculation by a structural engineer. We carry out the strengthening to that design, check the bond and document the result. That way, you and your engineer know exactly what is in the structure.

Our approach

How we strengthen a structure

Every strengthening job starts from a calculation. We work closely with your structural engineer and carry out the work to their design.

  1. 01

    Investigation and calculation

    We determine the condition and strength of the concrete. On that basis, the structural engineer calculates how much strengthening is needed.

    Concrete testing and inspection
  2. 02

    Repairing the substrate

    We repair damaged concrete first, because strengthening is only as strong as the substrate it is bonded to.

    Concrete repair
  3. 03

    Preparing the surface

    By blasting or grinding, we remove the weak top layer, so that the adhesive anchors into sound concrete.

    Blasting techniques
  4. 04

    Bonding the reinforcement

    Carbon laminates, fibre fabric or steel plates are applied to the element with a structural epoxy adhesive, exactly according to plan.

  5. 05

    Protection and finishing

    Where needed, we protect the strengthening with a coating against UV, fire or mechanical damage.

    Protective coatings
  6. 06

    Inspection

    Adhesion tests and a visual check confirm that the strengthening is properly bonded. You receive the documentation.

Frequently asked questions

Questions about bonded reinforcement

Your question is not listed? Ask us directly.

What is bonded reinforcement?

It is additional reinforcement that we bond to the outside of an existing element. Usually this means carbon laminates or carbon fibre fabric, sometimes aramid or steel. The adhesive makes the new reinforcement work together with the existing concrete, so that the element can carry more.

Why carbon and not steel?

Carbon is very strong, light and does not rust. It adds hardly any weight or thickness and is easy to install, even in tight spaces. Steel plates are sometimes a good alternative, for example when fire resistance is the deciding factor.

Is a calculation needed?

Yes. A structural engineer determines how much strengthening is needed and where it should go. If you do not yet have an engineer, we will put you in touch with an engineering firm we work with.

Can the building stay in use?

Usually, yes. No demolition is needed and the work zone remains limited. While the adhesive cures, the element must not be loaded more heavily. We agree on this with you in advance.

Is bonded reinforcement fire safe?

The adhesive loses strength at high temperatures. Where fire resistance is required, we provide fire protection or choose another system. That is part of the design.

How much does strengthening cost?

That depends on the required strength, the chosen material, the surface area and the accessibility. After a site visit, you will receive a clear, free quote with no obligation.
