Warning signs

When is a coating needed?

A coating is the last and often decisive layer between your structure and its environment. These are the situations in which a good coating makes the difference.

Rust on steel

Steel beams, stairs, railings and structures that rust lose strength and look neglected.

Water and road salt

Car park decks, balconies and facades that absorb water and salts start to deteriorate after a while.

Chemical exposure

Oil, acids and cleaning agents attack unprotected concrete and steel in workshops and industrial settings.

Slippery areas

Ramps, loading docks and stairs that become slippery in wet weather call for a slip resistant coating.

Why a coating?

Concrete and steel seem indestructible, but they share a common enemy: water, and everything it carries with it. Road salt on a car park deck, moisture in a facade or oil on a workshop floor slowly penetrate the material and attack it. A coating seals the surface. It keeps water, salts and chemicals out, protects steel against rust and makes a surface easier to clean. It is a relatively small investment that extends the life of a structure by years.

A dedicated system for every substrate

Concrete receives a coating that is waterproof yet open enough to vapour to let moisture escape from the core. On car park decks we choose a system that withstands road salt, tyres and cracking of the concrete.

Steel calls for a system that stops corrosion: a bonding primer with rust inhibiting pigments, followed by intermediate and top coats in a total thickness suited to the environment. Less is needed indoors in a dry hall than outdoors by the sea or in an industrial environment.

Brick and masonry usually receive a transparent, water repellent treatment that preserves their appearance, or an opaque coating for damaged or discoloured facades.

Preparation makes the difference

A coating rarely fails because of the product, but almost always because of the substrate. Loose rust, old paint, grease or a weak top layer cause the new coat to come away after a short time. That is why we blast or grind the substrate, repair damage and check moisture and temperature before we start. Only then does a coating have the chance to do what it was made for.

Colour, gloss and safety

A coating protects, but it also determines the appearance. We work in any colour, matt or gloss, and mark parking bays, walkways and zones in a contrasting shade. On ramps, stairs and loading docks we add slip resistance, so the surface stays safe in the rain too.

Our approach

How we apply a coating

The durability of a coating is largely determined before the first tin is opened: in the choice of system and the preparation.

  1. 01

    Choosing the system

    We choose the coating system based on the substrate, the load and the environment: indoors or outdoors, water, salt, chemicals or traffic.

  2. 02

    Preparing the substrate

    We remove rust, old paint and the weak top layer by blasting or grinding, so the coating bonds firmly.

    Blasting techniques
  3. 03

    Repairs

    We first repair concrete damage and rust pitting, so the coating goes onto a sound, even substrate.

    Concrete repair
  4. 04

    Primer

    A primer provides adhesion and protects steel against corrosion beneath the following coats.

  5. 05

    Intermediate and top coats

    We apply the intermediate coat and the top coat at the correct thickness, with the colour, gloss and slip resistance agreed.

  6. 06

    Inspection

    We measure the coating thickness and check the finish, so the coating does what it was chosen for.

Frequently asked questions

Questions about protective coatings

Your question is not listed? Ask us directly.

What is the difference between a coating and a poured floor?

A coating is a thin protective layer a few tenths of a millimetre thick. A poured floor is thicker, usually a few millimetres, and forms a new walking surface. For heavy loads we choose a poured floor, for protection and finish a coating.

How long does a coating last?

That depends on the system, the thickness, the preparation and the load. A well chosen and correctly applied coating lasts for many years. Where traffic is intensive, we schedule a maintenance coat.

Can a coating go over old paint?

Sometimes, if the old layer is firmly attached and compatible with the new coating. Usually we blast back to the substrate, because a coating never bonds better than the layer beneath it.

Are there slip resistant coatings?

Yes. By scattering or mixing grit into the layer, the coating gets a controlled roughness. That keeps ramps, docks and stairs safe, even in wet weather.

Can coatings be applied in winter?

Coatings need a minimum temperature and a maximum air humidity to cure properly. Indoors this is almost always possible. Outdoors we schedule the works in a suitable period.

How much does a coating cost?

That depends on the surface area, the condition of the substrate, the chosen system and access. After a site visit, you will receive a clear, free quote with no obligation.
